The Late Market — a judge's guide

Siki & Zana · Zoud GameLab, “Make FinLit Fun” · ~10–15 minutes · free in your browser

What this is: a financial-literacy game. You keep a stall open for one week, and a night market buys every invoice you leave. Defer once and a stall lights. The ending is what the street took.

How to play it so the street moves

  • Defer Harborne on Day 1. £2,400 is already on the street. A stall lights in that same beat, and the stallholder speaks. Chase it instead if you want the money in the till tomorrow morning — Day 3 is the same offer, and that chase lands after the week closes.
  • Leave Quayline's bill on Day 2. The till moves, and another stall lights. Paying it keeps the week open, and only yesterday's chase makes it affordable.
  • Name a floor at the Claims Desk, three times. The picture at the end is your floor against their three opening numbers.

What the ending shows

  • One large number: what the street took. Then two prices for the same wait — the street's, and the lawful one (UK statutory interest, or the Souq's contractual fee).
  • The bias is named only if your two Harborne choices differed. A week that chased both is told so, in one sentence.
  • Two markets. London uses pounds and the Late Payment of Commercial Debts Act. The Souq uses dirhams, a contractual late fee, and zakat on what you never collected. Same week. Pick either on the first screen.

Honest limits (stated in-game too)

  • One week is one week. The debrief names a tilt only from the two choices you actually made.
  • Fictional offers are labelled fictional; indicative ranges are labelled indicative. No provider is named or recommended; the game never moves money.
  • Audio is optional; every line is subtitled. Simple-language mode, adjustable text size, and reduced-motion are in Options.
